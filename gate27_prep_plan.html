Phase 1 — Foundation
June – July 2026
Months 1 & 2 · ~8 weeks
Goal: Rebuild the mathematical and logical foundation. Start the subjects that need the most time. Lock your daily routine.
June 2026
Routine + Maths + Discrete
Discrete Mathematics Linear Algebra General Aptitude
  • Week 1: Lock your daily schedule. Don't try to study — just build the habit of sitting down at fixed times. 3–4 hrs is fine this week.
  • Discrete Maths: Start with Set Theory, Relations, Functions, Propositional Logic. These are the easiest topics and give early confidence. Go through GATE PYQs from 2015–2025 for each topic as you finish it.
  • Linear Algebra: Matrices, determinants, eigenvalues, rank. Use Gilbert Strang's lectures or Goclasses. Practice 15–20 numericals daily — this is a marks-heavy topic that most people delay.
  • Aptitude: 15 questions daily minimum. Verbal + quant both. Never skip this — it's your free marks insurance.
  • End of June target: Finish Discrete Maths (sets, logic, relations, functions, graph theory basics) + Linear Algebra. Solve all PYQs for these topics.
Lock daily routine Discrete maths PYQs done Linear algebra numericals
July 2026
Discrete (finish) + TOC begins + Probability
TOC Probability & Stats Discrete Maths Aptitude
  • TOC — start early, go slow: Regular languages, DFA, NFA, minimization, regular expressions. This topic needs weeks of exposure — don't try to rush it. 1.5 hrs/day is enough to start.
  • Probability: Bayes theorem, conditional probability, distributions (Poisson, Binomial, Normal basics), expectation. These questions appear every year and are very scorable.
  • Discrete (finish): Graph theory (BFS/DFS on graphs, trees, spanning trees, Euler/Hamiltonian), Combinatorics, Recurrence relations. These are harder — take your time.
  • First mini-mock: Take a 30-question subject test on Discrete + Maths at end of July. Diagnose weak spots.
  • Corporate exams: Parallel prep is fine this month. Don't let it eat more than 1.5 hrs/day of GATE time.
TOC started (Regular languages done) Probability finished First mini-mock taken
02
Phase 2 — Core Subjects
August – September 2026
Months 3 & 4 · ~8 weeks
Goal: Cover OS, DBMS, Networks and finish TOC. These are your biggest unexplored mark sources. Heavy lifting phase.
August 2026
OS + TOC (CFL, PDA)
Operating Systems TOC continued DSA touch-up Aptitude daily
  • OS: Processes & threads, CPU scheduling (all algorithms + numericals), memory management (paging, segmentation, page replacement), deadlocks, file systems. This subject revives fast — 3–4 weeks is enough for a solid base. Galvin textbook or Goclasses OS is the reference.
  • OS numericals: Scheduling (Gantt charts, turnaround time, waiting time), page replacement (FIFO, LRU, Optimal), banker's algorithm — these appear every single year. Practice 10+ numericals per topic.
  • TOC continued: Context-free grammars, PDAs, CYK algorithm, ambiguity. This is where TOC gets harder — don't rush, understand each concept before moving on.
  • DSA: Don't fully drop it. 3–4 PYQ problems every 2 days to keep it warm. Focus on trees and graphs — most tested.
OS fully covered OS numericals practiced TOC up to CFL done
September 2026
DBMS + TOC (finish) + Networks begins
DBMS TOC — Turing machines Networks intro OS revision
  • DBMS: ER diagrams, relational model, SQL (joins, nested queries, aggregation), normalization (1NF to BCNF), transactions (ACID, serializability, concurrency control), indexing (B+ trees). This is very scorable — 3 weeks is enough. Do Ravindrababu Ravula's DBMS or Goclasses.
  • DBMS PYQs: SQL query tracing and normalization questions repeat patterns every year. After each topic, solve ALL PYQs for that topic before moving on.
  • TOC finish: Turing machines, decidability, reducibility, P vs NP. This is the hardest part of TOC — give it proper time. 2 weeks minimum. Don't memorize — understand what each class means.
  • Networks: Start with OSI model, TCP/IP, data link layer (framing, error detection, MAC). Just introduction this month — Networks gets its dedicated time in October.
  • Subject-wise mock: Take a full OS + DBMS test (25–30 questions) end of September. This is your first real assessment.
DBMS fully covered + PYQs done TOC fully finished OS + DBMS mock taken

Phase 3 — Complete Coverage
October – November 2026
Months 5 & 6 · ~8 weeks
Goal: Finish Networks, COA, Compiler Design. Complete the entire syllabus. Begin full-length mocks.
October 2026
Networks (full) + COA
Computer Networks COA DBMS revision First full mock
  • Networks: Physical + data link (error detection CRC, sliding window protocols, CSMA), network layer (IP addressing, subnetting, routing algorithms — Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford), transport layer (TCP vs UDP, congestion control, flow control), application layer (DNS, HTTP, FTP). Heavy on numericals — subnetting and sliding window are must-practice.
  • COA: Number systems, boolean algebra, combinational + sequential circuits, CPU organization (pipelining — this is critical, very frequently tested), memory hierarchy (cache — direct mapped, set associative, fully associative), I/O. Pipeline numericals and cache numericals appear almost every year.
  • First full mock: Take your first complete GATE mock (100 marks, 3 hours) in the last week of October. Don't panic at the score — it's a diagnostic. Analyze every wrong answer.
  • Revision habit: By now you have 5+ subjects covered. Spend 30 min/day on rotating revision of older subjects so they don't go cold.
Networks complete COA complete First full mock taken + analyzed
November 2026
Compiler Design + syllabus completion + mock cadence
Compiler Design All subjects rotating 2 full mocks
  • Compiler Design: Lexical analysis, parsing (LL, LR, SLR, LALR — know the differences and limitations), syntax directed translation, intermediate code generation, code optimization basics. This pairs well with TOC — same formal language thinking. 2.5 weeks is enough.
  • Compiler PYQs: Parsing questions are the most frequent — first/follow sets, parsing table construction. Practice these until they feel mechanical.
  • Syllabus complete checkpoint: By end of November every subject should have been covered at least once. This is critical — entering December without full coverage is a red flag.
  • Mock cadence: 2 full mocks this month. After each mock — spend as much time analyzing as you did taking it. Wrong answers and lucky correct guesses both need investigation.
  • Weak subject audit: After 2 mocks, you'll know which 2–3 subjects are consistently letting you down. Make a list — that becomes December's priority.
Entire syllabus covered ✓ Compiler design done 2 mocks taken + analyzed Weak subject list ready

Phase 4 — Intensive Revision
December 2026 – January 2027
Months 7 & 8 · ~8 weeks
Goal: Full revision of all subjects, mock every week, close weak subject gaps. Get your score consistently above 45 in mocks.
December 2026
Targeted revision + weekly mocks
All subjects Weekly full mocks Weak subjects targeted
  • Revision strategy: Don't re-read notes from scratch. Go directly to PYQs for each subject — what you can't solve shows you what to re-study. Much faster than passive re-reading.
  • Weekly mocks: One full mock every Sunday. Analyze Monday morning — categorize errors as (a) concept gap, (b) silly mistake, (c) time pressure. Each needs a different fix.
  • Weak subjects: Dedicate 2 hrs/day to your identified 2–3 weak areas. Don't spread time equally — fix the leaks.
  • PYQ bank: By December, you should be solving GATE PYQs from 2010–2025 for every subject. Track your accuracy per subject. Target 70%+ accuracy in strong subjects, 55%+ in weak ones.
  • Score target by end of December: Consistently 38–43 marks in full mocks. If you're hitting this, you're on track.
  • Mental health check: December is when prep fatigue peaks. Keep your daily break time sacred. One full day off per week — not optional.
4 full mocks taken Mock score 38–43 range PYQ 2010–2025 all subjects
January 2027
Final revision + mock peak + stabilization
All subjects — final sweep 2 mocks per week
  • First 3 weeks: 2 mocks per week. One on Wednesday, one on Sunday. This builds exam-day stamina — 3 hours of sustained concentration is a skill you need to practice.
  • Final subject sweep: Quick but complete pass through all subjects using your own short notes. If you don't have short notes by now, make them this month — 1 page per subject covering formulas, key concepts, tricky points.
  • Last week of January: Stop taking full mocks. Switch to subject-wise 30-min tests only. Your brain needs to consolidate, not absorb new patterns.
  • Score target by mid-January: Consistently 45–52 marks in full mocks. This is your real exam performance predictor.
  • Logistics: Admit card, exam center location, travel plan — sort all of this in January, not last minute.
  • Confidence calibration: You will hit bad mocks. A 38 after a 48 is normal — don't spiral. Track your average over 5 mocks, not individual scores.
Mock score consistently 45–52 Short notes for all subjects done Admit card + logistics sorted
05
Phase 5 — Exam Week
February 2027
Final 2 weeks · Pre-exam and exam day
Goal: Preserve what you've built. Don't try to learn new things. Stay sharp, stay calm.
Feb Week 1 — Wind down
Light revision only
  • Only look at your short notes — no new material, no new PYQs you haven't seen before.
  • One half-length mock (50 marks, 90 min) to keep your brain in exam mode — not a full 3-hour one.
  • Sleep 8 hours minimum. This week your brain is consolidating everything you've studied — sleep is literally the study.
  • Eat normally, stay hydrated, no junk food spikes. Sounds small — matters on exam day.
  • Visualize the exam hall, the interface, the first 30 minutes — mental rehearsal reduces anxiety.
Exam day strategy
Execute, don't perform
  • First 10 min: Read the full paper quickly. Mark questions as (a) do now, (b) do later, (c) skip. Don't start solving yet.
  • Order: Aptitude first (15 marks, easy win), then your strong subjects (DSA, Discrete, subjects you know cold), then medium, leave the hardest for last.
  • 1-mark questions: Don't spend more than 90 seconds on any 1-mark question. Move on.
  • 2-mark questions: Max 4–5 minutes per question. If stuck, mark and come back.
  • Negative marking: Only guess if you can eliminate 2 options confidently. Random guessing on 2-mark questions costs 0.67 marks — not worth it.
  • Last 20 min: Review marked questions. Don't second-guess answers you were confident about — change only if you spot a clear error.

Official GATE CS + GA Syllabus

Complete syllabus from the official GATE 2026 brochure (IIT Guwahati). Covers all 10 CS sections + General Aptitude.

Section 1: Engineering Mathematics Mathematics

Discrete Mathematics

Propositional and first order logic. Sets, relations, functions, partial orders and lattices. Monoids, Groups. Graphs: connectivity, matching, colouring. Combinatorics: counting, recurrence relations, generating functions.

Linear Algebra

Matrices, determinants, system of linear equations, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, LU decomposition.

Calculus

Limits, continuity and differentiability. Maxima and minima. Mean value theorem. Integration.

Probability and Statistics

Random variables. Uniform, normal, exponential, Poisson and binomial distributions. Mean, median, mode and standard deviation. Conditional probability and Bayes theorem.

Section 2: Digital Logic Core CS

Boolean algebra. Combinational and sequential circuits. Minimization. Number representations and computer arithmetic (fixed and floating point).

Section 3: Computer Organization and Architecture Core CS

Machine instructions and addressing modes. ALU, data‐path and control unit. Instruction pipelining, pipeline hazards. Memory hierarchy: cache, main memory and secondary storage; I/O interface (interrupt and DMA mode).

Section 4: Programming and Data Structures Core CS

Programming in C. Recursion. Arrays, stacks, queues, linked lists, trees, binary search trees, binary heaps, graphs.

Section 5: Algorithms Core CS

Searching, sorting, hashing. Asymptotic worst case time and space complexity. Algorithm design techniques: greedy, dynamic programming and divide‐and‐conquer. Graph traversals, minimum spanning trees, shortest paths.

Section 6: Theory of Computation Core CS

Regular expressions and finite automata. Context-free grammars and push-down automata. Regular and context-free languages, pumping lemma. Turing machines and undecidability.

Section 7: Compiler Design Core CS

Lexical analysis, parsing, syntax-directed translation. Runtime environments. Intermediate code generation. Local optimization. Data flow analyses: constant propagation, liveness analysis, common sub-expression elimination.

Section 8: Operating System Core CS

System calls, processes, threads, inter‐process communication, concurrency and synchronization. Deadlock. CPU and I/O scheduling. Memory management and virtual memory. File systems.

Section 9: Databases Core CS

ER‐model. Relational model: relational algebra, tuple calculus, SQL. Integrity constraints, normal forms. File organization, indexing (e.g., B and B+ trees). Transactions and concurrency control.

Section 10: Computer Networks Core CS

Concept of layering: OSI and TCP/IP Protocol Stacks; Basics of packet, circuit and virtual circuit-switching; Data link layer: framing, error detection, Medium Access Control, Ethernet bridging; Routing protocols: shortest path, flooding, distance vector and link state routing; Fragmentation and IP addressing, IPv4, CIDR notation, Basics of IP support protocols (ARP, DHCP, ICMP), Network Address Translation (NAT); Transport layer: flow control and congestion control, UDP, TCP, sockets; Application layer protocols: DNS, SMTP, HTTP, FTP, Email.

General Aptitude GA — 15 Marks

Verbal Aptitude

Basic English grammar: tenses, articles, adjectives, prepositions, conjunctions, verb-noun agreement, and other parts of speech. Basic vocabulary: words, idioms, and phrases in context. Reading and comprehension. Narrative sequencing.

Quantitative Aptitude

Data interpretation: data graphs (bar graphs, pie charts, and other graphs representing data), 2- and 3-dimensional plots, maps, and tables. Numerical computation and estimation: ratios, percentages, powers, exponents and logarithms, permutations and combinations, and series. Mensuration and geometry. Elementary statistics and probability.

Analytical Aptitude

Logic: deduction and induction. Analogy. Numerical relations and reasoning.

Spatial Aptitude

Transformation of shapes: translation, rotation, scaling, mirroring, assembling, and grouping. Paper folding, cutting, and patterns in 2 and 3 dimensions.

Things that kill GATE prep — watch for these
  • Studying a topic without solving PYQs for it immediately after — passive learning gives false confidence
  • Spending too much time on one subject and running out of time for others — stick to the monthly plan
  • Skipping mock analysis — taking mocks without analyzing them is the single biggest waste of prep time
  • Dropping aptitude because it "feels easy" — 15 marks of near-free marks is your baseline cushion
  • Comparing your mock scores to others online — irrelevant, demoralizing, and usually fake
  • Waiting to feel "ready" before starting mocks — start mocks in October even if you feel underprepared
  • Inconsistent sleep in the last month — your brain consolidates memory during sleep, not during study
